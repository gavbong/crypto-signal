## Overview

Lecture #5 extends the NDOG/NWOG framework to three additional trading windows: the Asian session (7:00–9:00 PM New York time), the London opening range (12:00–12:30 AM New York time), and the Friday TGIF retracement rule. ICT teaches the Turtle Soup pattern — a reversal off stop-hunt runs above/below relative equal highs/lows — as the week's new setup. The Opening Range (9:30–10:00 AM) is formally defined as a 30-minute algorithmic liquidity-engineering window, not a 15-minute window. ICT introduces Fibonacci standard-deviation extensions (negative 1.0 and negative 1.5) as a measured-move target methodology, and explains chart annotation discipline: why clean (annotation-free) charts should be the goal once the student has experience, while annotations remain appropriate for beginners.

## Key Concepts

### Asian Session Trading Window
- **Active window:** 7:00 PM to 9:00 PM New York local time.
- This is ICT's **least preferred** time of day for trading personally, but it is teachable and uses identical tools.
- Applicable to both indices (NQ, ES) and Forex.
- The 6:00 PM open creates the **New Day Opening Gap (NDOG)** — same construction as taught in prior lectures.
- At 6:00 PM, annotate the new NDOG. Then **wait for 7:00 PM** — that is the algorithmic trigger point for the Asian session. Do not trade immediately at 6:00 PM.
- If price is completely lethargic at 6:00 PM and does not move away from the NDOG going into 7:00 PM, do NOT trade. "That's typically telling you that Asia is going to be dull — go watch a movie with your spouse."
- The algorithm "comes online" with buy/sell programs beginning right at 7:00 PM or just after.

### NDOG Protocol for Asian Session (6 PM → 7 PM Engineering Sequence)
The five-step sequence ICT teaches for Asian setup identification:

1. **Mark the NDOG** at 6:00 PM (high = max(5PM close, 6PM open); low = min(5PM close, 6PM open); CE = midpoint).
2. **Watch price move away** from the NDOG — this is the first required condition. Price must leave the gap, not sit inside it.
3. **Watch price take a short-term high OR low** (establishing initial buy side or sell side liquidity). This happens in both directions before 7:00 PM.
4. **Watch price cross back over the NDOG** (color outside the lines) — e.g., if it first ran above, it must cross back below to establish initial sell-side liquidity on the opposing side.
5. At **7:00 PM**: price retraces back to touch the NDOG high or low. This touch confirms the setup is alive.
6. Entry: after the 7:00 PM touch confirms, look for a **BISI/FVG displacement** away from the NDOG as the breakaway signal.

- Key phrase: "We now have initial buy side liquidity and initial sell side liquidity — now you're waiting for 7 o'clock."
- If at 7:00 PM price revisits and confirms the NDOG without making a new extreme through the initial reference highs/lows → momentum trade setup is valid.

### Initial Buy Side and Sell Side Liquidity (NDOG Context)
- After the 6:00 PM NDOG is formed and price moves to establish the first short-term High and short-term Low, those two extremes are labeled:
  - **Initial Buy Side Liquidity** = the first short-term HIGH created after 6:00 PM (buy stops rest above it).
  - **Initial Sell Side Liquidity** = the first short-term LOW created after 6:00 PM (sell stops rest below it).
- These two reference points are **day-long references** — not just for Asia. Price will return to these levels throughout the New York session.
- "The initial sell-side and buy-side liquidity reference points formed at or after the 6 PM open are key all day."

### Breakaway Gap (Confirmation Signal)
- A **BISI (bullish FVG)** that price does NOT return to after a displacement = "breakaway gap."
- If you observe the FVG left open (no wick overlap, no body retracement) for 2+ candles after the displacement → price is confirming a strong directional run.
- ICT specifically wants to see **speed and magnitude** ("nitrous oxide") in the displacement candle.
- "Every displacement does not have to see it trade back down into — it's better for your trades to not see them return into the bid-balance/sell-efficiency at all."

### Turtle Soup (Reversal Pattern)
- Origin: Named after Richard Dennis's "Turtle Traders" — Linda Raschke and Larry Connors described the inverse concept in the book *Street Smarts* (c. mid-1990s, ~$175 at release).
- ICT's version: a **reversal off a stop-hunt run** above relative equal highs or below relative equal lows.
- **Turtle Soup Long:** Price sweeps below a short-term low (taking out sell-side liquidity / stop-loss orders), then reverses back upward.
- **Turtle Soup Short:** Price sweeps above a relative equal high or old high (taking out buy-side stops), fails to hold above, then drops back into range.
- NOT based on a 20-day high/low breakout (Dennis's original system). ICT uses **relative equal highs/lows** or **old swing highs/lows** — any level where retail stops are clearly resting.
- Key qualifier: "I don't look at a 20-day high — I'd like to see retail resistance pegged and then reverse and then look for something that would take me to the other side of the spectrum."
- A Turtle Soup failure looks like: price runs above buy side → doesn't continue → immediately drops back below. If it **holds** above (with speed and magnitude) then it is NOT a Turtle Soup — it is a legitimate breakout.

### Opening Range (Algorithmic, 30-Minute Definition)
- **Opening Range = 9:30 AM to 10:00 AM Eastern.** ICT explicitly corrects the common retail belief that the opening range is 15 minutes.
- "There is no 15-minute opening range — opening range algorithmically is 30 minutes."
- During this 30-minute window the algorithm does the **same thing as the 6:00 PM NDOG launch**: it engineers liquidity by establishing initial buy-side and sell-side reference points.
- A green 9:30 candle where the open = the low of the candle → bullish opening; a red candle where the open = the high → bearish opening.
- The opening range high and low (highest high + lowest low of the 9:30–10:00 AM 30-minute span) are the reference levels to annotate.
- Price will refer back to these opening range extremes throughout the day session, just as it refers back to NDOG levels.

### Gap Fill Probability at the Open (Regular Trading Hours Gap)
- When there is a **large gap lower** on the RTH (regular trading hours) open vs. the prior day 4:15 PM close:
  - **~70% probability** price will trade back to the **midpoint of the gap** (CE of the gap).
  - ICT's rule: "I like this generally — even if the market's going to be bearish and continue going lower after a big gap lower, there's generally a 70% chance it's going to go back to mid-gap."
  - This is a tradeable setup on its own: enter long at/near gap low, target the midpoint.
  - A full gap closure is less reliable; focus on the midpoint (CE).

### London Opening Range
- **London Opening Range = 12:00 AM to 12:30 AM New York local time** (30-minute range).
- This is the direct analog of the 9:30–10:00 AM NY Opening Range, but for the London session.
- Take the **highest high and lowest low** within the 12:00–12:30 AM window.
- Extend those levels forward to the right; price will refer back to them during the London kill zone and into the NY open.
- The NDOG formed at 6:00 PM the prior evening remains in force; the London Opening Range works alongside it.
- "What I'm describing here — all you're doing is watching what price is doing; the only difference is where we have the new day opening gap, for London what we're going to be looking at is the 30 minutes after 12:00 to 12:30 New York local time because that is your opening range for London."

### TGIF — Friday Weekly Retracement Rule
- After a strong directional week (price moved predominantly one direction from Sunday open to Thursday/Friday), the market has a tendency to **retrace** on Friday.
- **Retracement target range: 20%–30% of the full weekly range** (from weekly low to weekly high).
- Formula: `Weekly Range = Weekly High − Weekly Low; TGIF Target = Weekly High − (Weekly Range × 0.20 to 0.30)`
- ICT acknowledges that up to 40% retracements exist but states he has not found a way to teach the precise rule for those.
- The rationale: institutional players do not want to hold risk over the weekend, so they reduce positions, pulling price back.
- This is **not a daily bias reversal** — it is a partial retracement within an intact weekly trend.
- "It wouldn't unravel anything even if it's long-term bullish — 30% of the weekly range giving up is normal."
- Friday TGIF rule activates when: (1) the week has been clearly one-sided, and (2) no new significant economic data is scheduled for Friday that would drive a fresh directional move.

### Measured Move Targets (Fibonacci Standard Deviation Extensions)
- ICT's preferred targeting methodology for exits:
  - Take the **prior price leg** (the swing leg immediately before the entry move): measure from the prior leg's high down to its low.
  - Apply Fibonacci retracement from that leg's high to low.
  - **Negative 1.0 (−1.0):** A full measured move — adds the entire leg distance to the launch point. This is the "classic measured move" or "low-hanging fruit" target. ICT says Tom, Dick, and Harry use this, so it is the minimum expectation.
  - **Negative 1.5 (−1.5):** Adds 1.5× the leg distance from the launch point. This is where ICT expects price to reach when conditions are strong ("she's going to run").
  - ICT's preferred **gray area exit**: somewhere between −1.0 and −1.5. "I want to be in that little gray area between where I think it ultimately may reach for and the certain measured-move idea."
- Practical rule: "If you're looking for a bullish run, look at a price leg prior to the run you're in as a long, do a measurement, and that's your measured move."
- ICT exits the live trade at approximately the −1.05 level (between −1.0 and −1.5), satisfied because precision in targeting is not a steady diet: "I'm pursuing perfect — it's always been a target I know it's going to evade me."

### Chart Annotation Discipline
- In the beginning: it is **OK and expected** to have all annotations visible on the chart. This is the study phase.
- Long-term goal: **trade with a clean chart**, key levels on a notepad (paper or tablet) external to the chart.
- Reason: annotated lines on a chart can cause a trader to stubbornly hold positions because "I have these lines, it's going to do this."
- "In the beginning I say it's OK for you to have the annotations on your chart initially — that's fine. But once you understand what you're doing, you want to try to gravitate to where you're trying to have as little as possible."
- ICT uses a Samsung Galaxy Note to write down numeric key levels before each session. The notepad is not on screen.
- NDOG annotation rule: if the gap is **less than 10 handles**, eyeball the CE and quadrants; do not draw lines unless studying. If **more than 20 handles**, always annotate CE explicitly.

### SMT Divergence — Instrument Hierarchy and Weighting
- NQ vs. ES divergence = **most valid** SMT signal. These are the two primary instruments for SMT.
- YM (Dow) divergence = **lower reliability** because YM is only 30 stocks ("the dirty 30"). ICT does not trade YM directly.
- ICT's rule for YM divergence: "Any divergence that I see in the Dow I take it with a grain of salt."
- ICT's rule for NQ vs. ES divergence: if NQ makes a higher high and ES does NOT confirm → take a partial off any long trade immediately. "That's a more valid SMT divergence — it's indicating there's potentially much more weakness that may not be visible to other traders."
- When the Dow diverges against NQ and ES moving together → do not close the trade entirely; use caution and consider partials only.

### Previous Day High and Previous Day Low
- "The majority of your big nice setups are going to form around old highs and old lows — meaning previous day high and previous day low."
- This was ICT's first teaching point on Baby Pips in 2010 and remains foundational.
- In the Asian session and London session, price returning to the previous day high or previous day low is a predictable event worth marking on the chart before each session.

### Relative Equal Highs and Lows (REH / REL) — Turtle Soup Context
- Two highs at approximately the same price level = **relative equal highs** → buy-side liquidity pool.
- Two lows at approximately the same price level = **relative equal lows** → sell-side liquidity pool.
- Retail traders buy the breakout above REH or sell the breakdown below REL; their stops are on the other side. The algorithm harvests these stops.
- After a REH is swept, the entry is either (a) the subsequent FVG or volume imbalance immediately below the sweep, or (b) an order block just below the sweep candle.
- Annotation: above = "Buy Side"; below = "Sell Side." Use small text, top-left and bottom-left of the line.

### No Medium/High Impact News = Choppy Session Risk
- On days with **no medium or high impact economic events** in the morning session (pre-10:00 AM):
  - The market "can be aimless, choppy, not as clear."
  - It can still have big runs due to external stimuli (geopolitical events, etc.).
  - Best approach: reduce trade frequency, wait for very clean setups only, and be willing to sit on hands all morning.
- Reference: ICT explicitly contrasts Wednesday (no news, choppy) with Tuesday (employment data, clear directional run).

### Electronic vs. Regular Trading Hours Chart Setting
- **Electronic Trading Hours** chart shows all 23 hours of trading (6 PM–5 PM next day, minus the 5–6 PM break). Used for NDOG/NWOG analysis.
- **Regular Trading Hours** chart shows only 9:30 AM–4:15 PM. Used when analyzing the RTH open gap and opening range (9:30–10:00 AM).
- The two charts look **completely different** — ICT emphasizes students must toggle intentionally and understand which view they are using.
- RTH gap = the difference between the prior day's 4:15 PM closing price and the 9:30 AM opening price on the current day.

## Setups Taught

### Setup 1: Asian Session NDOG Reversal (7 PM–9 PM, New York Time)
**Conditions:**
1. NDOG formed at 6:00 PM; gap has moved away from open price.
2. Price has established initial buy-side and sell-side liquidity (one short-term high AND one short-term low created after 6:00 PM).
3. Price has crossed the NDOG in at least one direction (coloring outside the lines).
4. At 7:00 PM: price retraces back to touch the NDOG high or low (not below initial sell-side low).
5. A BISI or volume imbalance is left open (breakaway gap) pointing in the trade direction.

**Entry:**
- After the 7:00 PM NDOG retest, buy the pullback to the BISI or volume imbalance.
- Enter **below the body of the down-close OB candle** (in the discount wick portion of the OB candle). Worst-case entry: the open of the OB candle.
- Example: ICT entered at 18,559.75 when the OB candle close was 18,560.00 — buying below the close.

**Stop:**
- Below the **Consequent Encroachment (CE) of the NDOG** (not just below the OB candle close).
- After the initial buy-side liquidity (the short-term high from the 6:00 PM range) is taken out, move stop to **break even** at minimum, or to the top of the NDOG.

**Targets:**
- **Primary:** Initial buy-side liquidity pool (the short-term high formed after 6:00 PM).
- **Secondary:** Measured move (negative 1.0 of the prior price leg = full leg duplication from launch; negative 1.5 for extended run).
- ICT's gray-area exit: between −1.0 and −1.5 Fibonacci extension of the prior leg.

**Invalidation:**
- Price trades back below the initial sell-side liquidity low (the short-term low formed after 6:00 PM) → no trade.
- If the NDOG is crossed and never reclaimed before 7:00 PM → no trade for the Asian session.

### Setup 2: Turtle Soup Reversal (Any Session)
**Conditions:**
1. Clear relative equal highs OR a previous day/week high that is "smooth" (untested, obvious).
2. Price approaches that level from below (buy side) or above (sell side).
3. Price displaces through the level, taking out all stop orders above (or below).
4. Displacement is NOT followed by speed and magnitude continuation (i.e., it fades back into range).

**Entry:**
- Short: after a sweep above REH, look for a SIBI (bearish FVG) or volume imbalance inside a bearish OB that forms on the retracement candle immediately after the sweep.
- Long: after a sweep below REL, look for a BISI or volume imbalance inside a bullish OB.

**Stop:**
- Short: 1 tick above the sweep high (above the stop-hunt candle's high).
- Long: 1 tick below the sweep low.

**Targets:**
- The opposing side liquidity: if shorting after a REH sweep, target REL or old lows below.
- Measured move: negative 1.0 or 1.5 of the run up (prior bullish leg).

**Invalidation:**
- Price sweeps above the REH and immediately continues upward with speed and magnitude (not a fade) → it is a genuine breakout, not a Turtle Soup. "If it goes up and then goes right back in because what that was is a fade — that's your Turtle Soup. What we're looking for is nitrous oxide."

---

### Setup 3: RTH Open Gap Fill to Midpoint (9:30 AM)
**Conditions:**
1. RTH (regular trading hours) chart shows a significant gap lower or higher between prior 4:15 PM close and current 9:30 AM open.
2. Gap is large enough to be visible on the chart (more than a few handles).

**Entry:**
- Long (gap down): enter near the 9:30 open low / opening candle's discount zone.
- No complex confirmation needed beyond the gap itself and the probability baseline.

**Stop:**
- Below the 9:30 opening candle low (or below the first significant low formed in the opening range, 9:30–10:00 AM).

**Target:**
- **Midpoint (CE) of the RTH gap** = prior 4:15 PM close − (gap range × 0.50).
- ICT states ~70% probability of reaching midpoint. Full gap closure is secondary and less reliable.

**Invalidation:**
- If price opens and immediately accelerates further in the gap direction (i.e., an extension, not a retracement) → skip the midpoint trade. The other 30% of the time the gap expands.

---

### Setup 4: Friday TGIF Weekly Retracement Short
**Conditions:**
1. The week has been clearly one-sided (predominantly up or predominantly down) from Sunday open.
2. It is Friday, and there is no significant medium/high impact news scheduled for Friday morning.
3. Friday opens near the extreme of the weekly range (near the high for a bullish week).

**Entry:**
- Short from a premium SIBI or inversion FVG near the weekly high, or from initial buy-side liquidity formed Friday morning.
- Alternatively, wait for the 10:00 AM Silver Bullet window to form a short setup.

**Stop:**
- Above the weekly high (1–3 ticks).

**Targets:**
- **20%–30% of the weekly range retraced downward:**
  `TGIF Low = Weekly High − (Weekly Range × 0.20)` to `Weekly High − (Weekly Range × 0.30)`
- Take partial at 20%; let runner go to 30%.

**Invalidation:**
- If the week was balanced (up and down, no clear directional bias) → TGIF rule does not apply.
- If a major news catalyst is scheduled for Friday (e.g., NFP) → TGIF rule is secondary to the news driver.

## What To Avoid

1. **Do not trade immediately at 6:00 PM.** Price must first move away from the NDOG, establish initial liquidity reference points, and cross the NDOG before the 7:00 PM trigger. Entering before this sequence plays out is premature.
2. **Do not trade the Asian session if price is lethargic at 6:00 PM.** "If it's real lethargic and lackluster at six o'clock and doesn't really do anything going into 7 o'clock — go watch a movie with your spouse or go exercise and go to bed early."
3. **Do not mistake a genuine breakout for a Turtle Soup.** If price sweeps above REH and continues with speed and magnitude (nitrous oxide), it is a breakout, not a fade. "If it goes up there and then goes right back in — that's your Turtle Soup. If it's got speed and distance, let it run."
4. **Do not use a 15-minute opening range.** "There is no 15-minute opening range — opening range algorithmically is 30 minutes, 9:30 to 10:00."
5. **Do not assume a gap will close completely.** The 70% probability is for midpoint (CE), not full closure. Trading to full closure adds significant risk for marginal reward.
6. **Do not apply TGIF on a balanced week.** The TGIF rule requires a clearly one-sided weekly move to be valid. If the week has chopped both directions, the rule does not apply.
7. **Do not trust YM (Dow) divergence as a primary signal.** The Dow is only 30 stocks; it diverges frequently and misleadingly. Use NQ vs. ES divergence as the valid SMT signal. "I take it with a grain of salt."
8. **Do not rush to remove chart annotations.** "Having them on you know once you understand what you're doing you want to try to gravitate to that — but don't think it's a superpower to trade naked without experience. It will slow you down."
9. **Do not trade more than one setup per session.** "I want you to have the ability to sit still — that is what messes everybody up, everybody does the wrong thing by trying to be more active than they should." Once a setup triggers and you are in a trade, stop looking for additional entries.
10. **Do not hold positions into uncertain macro events or over weekends when geopolitical risk is elevated.** "I don't want to be holding anything overnight — I want to be trading very large [conviction], not taking more trades than I should." Risk-off environments make Friday TGIF more aggressive; reduce hold time, do not overnight.
11. **Do not target only the measured −1.0 move.** It is the minimum expectation; be prepared for −1.5 when conditions are strong (speed, magnitude, breakaway gap). Exiting entirely at −1.0 when a −1.5 is valid is leaving money on the table. Use partials: take half at −1.0, let rest run to −1.5.
12. **Do not use classic support/resistance as a primary method.** Lines on price levels without the context of liquidity engineering (where are the stops?) and time windows will fail repeatedly. "I'm challenging someone else to go on a live stream and call the only support/resistance levels that are going to work — do yourself."

## Algorithmic Notes

### Asian Session Qualification Check
```python
# At 6:00 PM: construct NDOG
NDOG_HIGH = max(close_5PM, open_6PM)
NDOG_LOW  = min(close_5PM, open_6PM)
NDOG_CE   = (NDOG_HIGH + NDOG_LOW) / 2
gap_size  = NDOG_HIGH - NDOG_LOW
# Annotate CE only if gap_size >= 20 handles; eyeball for smaller gaps

# Track initial liquidity reference points (created between 6PM and 7PM)
init_buy_side  = max(highs_6PM_to_7PM)   # highest high after NDOG move-away
init_sell_side = min(lows_6PM_to_7PM)    # lowest low after NDOG move-away

# At 7:00 PM candle: check conditions
price_touched_NDOG_at_7PM = (current_price >= NDOG_LOW and current_price <= NDOG_HIGH)
price_did_not_breach_init_sell = (current_price > init_sell_side)
# If both true → setup is live for Asian session entry

if price_touched_NDOG_at_7PM and price_did_not_breach_init_sell:
    session_bias = BULLISH if current_price > NDOG_CE else BEARISH
    # Look for BISI / volume imbalance entry on 1m or 15s chart
```

### Measured Move Target (Fibonacci −1.0 / −1.5)
```python
# Prior price leg = the swing BEFORE the entry move
prior_leg_high = swing_high_before_entry
prior_leg_low  = swing_low_before_entry
leg_range      = prior_leg_high - prior_leg_low

# For a bullish measured move (launching from prior_leg_high):
target_neg1_0  = prior_leg_high + leg_range           # −1.0 extension
target_neg1_5  = prior_leg_high + (leg_range * 1.5)   # −1.5 extension
target_gray_area = (target_neg1_0 + target_neg1_5) / 2  # ICT's preferred exit zone

# If prior buy-side liquidity pool exists above:
# Confirm target_neg1_0 clears that pool — if not, target is the pool itself
```
